THN and EHU

THN is Hume's A Treatise of Human Nature, cited by book, part and section (THN 1.4.2 is Book 1, Part 4, Section 2). EHU is Hume's An Enquiry concerning Human Understanding, the first Enquiry, cited by section. A number given with “¶” is a paragraph number within a section. These pages cite sections and paragraphs, not the pages of a printed edition.

Used on: By Hume section

Catalogue record

One record of the library's catalogue as transcribed and identified in the UQAM edition (Projet La Flèche, Université du Québec à Montréal), taken by the project in March 2026.

Used on: Catalogue

Work (indexed work)

A work the project selected from the catalogue for processing, or a contextual text studied beside it; every indexed work is listed in the works index.

Used on: Works

Witness

The particular copy, usually a scan, from which a working text was made. A work can have several (volumes, or an earlier and a later text); each work page lists them, and a text's page states its source where it is recorded.

Used on: Works

Working text

One machine-read text made from one scan; a work can have several (volumes, or an earlier and a later ingestion), and only current ones are counted.

It has not been verified against the page images, it contains recognition errors, and it is not an edition.

Used on: Working texts

Source preview

Four works were published earlier in another format, as source previews: a reading text with page anchors, prepared and approved one by one. They stay at their addresses.

They were approved one by one for public display in July 2026, and their page anchors follow the project's normalized text layer. They have not been verified against the page images and are not editions.

Used on: Working texts

Project record

The identifier under which the project's records hold an item, such as a comparison (sc:LF:…) or a zone recorded by the principal investigator (cz:LF:…). It is given as text, not as a link.

Used on: Comparisons

Comparison record

Each comparison pairs one or more paragraphs of a French book with a passage of Hume: one or more paragraphs, or, where the nomination gives no paragraph, a section.

Used on: Comparisons

Review status

The status of a comparison in the project's records: selected by the principal investigator as warranting investigation (recorded classification: strong); selected by the principal investigator as warranting investigation (recorded classification: moderate); model-proposed, awaiting assessment; model-proposed, not pursued.

Used on: Comparisons

Selection by the principal investigator (the records' term 'confirmed')

In the project's records these comparisons are entered as 'confirmed' by the principal investigator. The entry means that the principal investigator selected the comparison as warranting investigation; it is not a verification of the passage-level analysis.

An outer ring records that the principal investigator selected the comparison as warranting investigation; it says nothing about the model reading shown by the fill.

Used on: Comparisons

Strong and moderate

The classification, strong or moderate, recorded with a selection by the principal investigator. The pages show it as the recorded classification.

Used on: Comparisons

Model contextual reading

For 29 of the 53 comparisons, one model analyst was given the proposed paragraphs together with seven paragraphs of the French text on either side and the section of Hume in which the proposed paragraph stands, and was asked to reach its own judgment, including a negative one.

AI-assisted reading (one model analyst, one pass; not human-verified)

Used on: Method

Passage level and doctrine level

It judged two things separately. At passage level: whether these paragraphs support a comparison with each other. At doctrine level: whether the doctrine or tradition the French passage belongs to supports a comparison with Hume, even where the passage itself does not.

Used on: Method

Substantive, partial, topical, unsupported

The ratings the analyst assigned are: substantive (in the analyst's judgment, a specific argumentative relationship that survives close reading); partial (real but narrower than or different from what was proposed); topical (the same subject without a shared argument); unsupported (the texts do not bear the comparison); they are not an independent confirmation.

'Substantive' and 'partial' are ratings assigned in an AI-assisted reading; they do not establish historical influence and have not been independently verified. Objections marked 'answered' or 'conceded' record the analyst's assessment of its own reading, not an independent adjudication.

Used on: Comparisons

Stance

Stance is recorded as support, opposition, qualification, different target or unclear.

Used on: Comparisons

Nominated Hume passage and analyst's preferred passage

The nominated Hume passage is the passage of Hume proposed when the comparison was entered; it is given by section only where the nomination gives no paragraph. The analyst's preferred passage is the passage of Hume that the model analyst, reading in context, named instead; where the two differ, the comparisons page says so and the comparison is still listed under the nominated section.

Used on: Comparisons, By Hume section

Putative zone and zone recorded by the principal investigator

A zone is a region of repeated or localised convergence between two texts. It may be asymmetric, overlapping, many-to-one or many-to-many. For two stretches of Régis, one model analyst read a longer run of the French text against the Hume section and listed every place where a specific step is shared, together with everything on each side that has no counterpart. A stretch is called a zone here when it has at least three links rated partial or substantive, involving at least three paragraphs of the French text and at least two of Hume. That threshold is a working convention fixed before the readings.

A zone is “putative” until the principal investigator records it.

Recording by the principal investigator is a decision to treat the terrain as worth investigation; it does not add evidence shown on these pages. The putative zones show a full contextual reading; the recorded zones show one comparison not read in context.

The studies and the project's records call a zone a 'contact zone'; it is the same object.

Used on: Zones

Study status and result

The status describes the record of a study, the result its outcome: a study can be 'not completed' while its record is 'current'.

Statuses: current, corrected, superseded, withdrawn, closed without a result. Results: positive, negative, inconclusive, mixed, not completed, descriptive.

Used on: Studies, Corrections

Text status

Working text already public as an approved preview; working machine-read text published on this site; not published on this site at present; no machine-read text.

Used on: Works

Paragraph numbers and printed pages

Numbers given as “p” followed by a number are paragraph numbers of the project's machine-read text of the French book, not page numbers of the printed book. Numbers given with “¶” are paragraph numbers within a section of Hume.

Used on: Method

Comparison, not a historical relation

A comparison records a textual relation proposed between passages. What these pages do not establish about any historical relation is stated at the head of every page.

Used on: Method
