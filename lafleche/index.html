Claim ceiling. This site presents approved source previews, catalogue materials, computational routing metadata, and review records to support scholarly inspection. It does not claim that Hume read, used, borrowed from, contacted, depended on, or was influenced by any listed text.

Books recorded in the catalogue of the library of the Jesuit college at La Flèche, the town where Hume lived from 1735 to 1737, are compared passage by passage with Hume's texts. Each comparison pairs one or more paragraphs of a French book with a passage of Hume: one or more paragraphs, or, where the nomination gives no paragraph, a section. A zone is a region of repeated or localised convergence between a book and a section of Hume.

These pages record textual relations that may warrant historical investigation. They are not evidence that Hume read, used, borrowed from, recalled or replied to any of these books.

Terms: see the glossary

The catalogue of the library on which these pages rest was compiled in December 1776 and January 1777, some forty years after Hume lived in the town of La Flèche (1735–1737), and after the Jesuits had ceased to run the college. A book listed in it was held by the library at that later date; the catalogue does not by itself show that the book, or that edition, was there in Hume's time.

Source: Projet La Flèche, Université du Québec à Montréal — 'À propos de l'édition' (https://projetlafleche.uqam.ca/a-propos-edition) and 'Histoire de la bibliothèque' (https://projetlafleche.uqam.ca/histoire-de-la-bibliotheque).

What these pages make possible: locating passages in the books of the library that correspond, in argument or in problem, to passages in Hume; seeing where such passages differ, often substantively; tracing problems the two sides share; and giving historians specific places to begin an investigation.

For example, Régis sets out criteria for the credibility of testimony and applies them to Christian historical claims; Hume also asks about the credibility of witnesses, but weighs the testimony against how unusual the reported event is, as uniform experience judges it. The overlap is in the structure of the two arguments, alongside an important philosophical disagreement. The analyst rated none of the links as specific to Régis rather than common to the seventeenth-century genre of proving Christianity from attested facts, so the example shows what a comparison can reveal, not that Hume was answering Régis. About the catalogue

This project works from the 1776–1777 catalogue of the library of the former Jesuit college at La Flèche, as transcribed in the UQAM edition, as a scholarly working environment for studying the intellectual setting associated with Hume's residence at La Flèche in the 1730s. It combines catalogue orientation, approved source previews, method records, and review-workflow materials. The catalogue dates from 1776–1777 (see above); it is not a record of what Hume read or used.
