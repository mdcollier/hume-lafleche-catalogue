How these pages were made

What these pages are, where the comparisons come from, and what was and was not checked.

Claim ceiling. This site presents approved source previews, catalogue materials, computational routing metadata, and review records to support scholarly inspection. It does not claim that Hume read, used, borrowed from, contacted, depended on, or was influenced by any listed text.

These pages record textual relations that may warrant historical investigation. They are not evidence that Hume read, used, borrowed from, recalled or replied to any of these books.

What these pages are. A working record of comparisons between books recorded in the catalogue of the library of the Jesuit college at La Flèche and the philosophical writings of David Hume, who lived at La Flèche from 1735 to 1737. A book's entry in the catalogue is a fact about the library's holdings; these pages do not establish when a book entered the library. Each comparison pairs one or more paragraphs of a French book with one or more paragraphs of Hume. The pages record textual relations that may warrant historical investigation. They are not evidence that Hume read, used, borrowed from, recalled or replied to any of these books.

Where the comparisons come from. Comparisons were first proposed by computational search over machine-read texts and by model reading in earlier stages of the project. That search is computational routing evidence, not evidence that Hume read, used or was influenced by a text. Some comparisons were then confirmed by the principal investigator as relations warranting investigation, with a strength of strong or moderate; others await assessment or were not pursued.

Contextual reading. For 29 of the 53 comparisons, one model analyst was given the proposed paragraphs together with seven paragraphs of the French text on either side and the section of Hume in which the proposed paragraph stands, and was asked to reach its own judgment, including a negative one. The analyst was not told whether a comparison had been confirmed. It judged two things separately. At passage level: whether these paragraphs support a comparison with each other. At doctrine level: whether the doctrine or tradition the French passage belongs to supports a comparison with Hume, even where the passage itself does not. The words used are: substantive, a specific argumentative relationship that survives close reading; partial, real but narrower than or different from what was proposed; topical, the same subject without a shared argument; unsupported, the texts do not bear the comparison. Stance is recorded as support, opposition, qualification, different target or unclear.

Zones. A contact zone is a region of repeated or localised convergence between two texts. It may be asymmetric, overlapping, many-to-one or many-to-many. For two stretches of Régis, one model analyst read a longer run of the French text against the Hume section and listed every place where a specific step is shared, together with everything on each side that has no counterpart. A stretch is called a zone here when it has at least three links rated partial or substantive, involving at least three paragraphs of the French text and at least two of Hume. That threshold is a working convention fixed before the readings. A zone is "putative" until the principal investigator records it.

Wording. On zone pages, the summary, the description of the pattern, the statement of what the reading does not show, and the labels of the stages in the figure were written for display from the analyst's reading, and are labelled as such. Elsewhere the analyst's own summaries are shown. In a small number of them, words quoted from the texts were replaced by description, so that no passage of either text is printed on these pages, and wording was made comparative where it could have been read as saying that Hume was replying to a particular book. One of an analyst's objections was shortened where it went beyond the two texts.

What was checked. Every quotation an analyst relied on was compared mechanically with the text it was taken from, and all were identical. That check establishes that the analyst quoted accurately. It does not establish that a quotation supports the conclusion drawn from it.

What was not checked. No reading here has been reviewed by a second reader. None has been checked against the secondary literature, so nothing on these pages should be taken as a claim that a comparison is new. The French texts are machine-read and have not been verified against page images; marginal notes and running heads are sometimes read into the text. Editions have not been compared: where the catalogue copy and the text read are different printings, the work's page says so. Doctrine-level judgments rest partly on the analyst's general knowledge of the tradition and not only on the text in front of it. Hume's sections were not always read whole.

Paragraph numbers. Numbers given as "p" followed by a number are paragraph numbers of the project's machine-read text of the French book, not page numbers of the printed book. Numbers given with "¶" are paragraph numbers within a section of Hume. For comparisons that have not been read in context, the Hume passage proposed is given by section only.

Disagreement. Where a model reading and an earlier confirmation by the principal investigator differ, both are shown and neither corrects the other.

The comparisons are on the comparisons page, the zones on the zones page, and the books on the works page.

Working texts and their sources

Each machine-read text published here was made from one scan, and the scan is tied to a copy by a published record. For a Gallica item, that record is the record of the BnF's general catalogue that links the item to the BnF's own holdings: it shows that the scan is of a copy in the BnF's collection, but where it lists several physical holdings it does not say which of them was scanned. For an Internet Archive item, the record is the Archive's own record of the item. Texts whose source is held elsewhere, or is not recorded, have not been looked up in this way.

A text published here may be made from an Internet Archive item whose own record carries an explicit public-domain statement; items without such a statement are not published on that basis.

The date and imprint shown for a scanned copy come from those records. They have not been checked against title pages.

Before a text was published, three things were read: its opening, its close, and every paragraph that an automatic check had flagged for scanning boilerplate or for modern dates.

Texts are published here in selected batches, and not every text the project holds will be published. A work without a published text here is not thereby described as unsuitable or unavailable. The published texts are on the texts page.

For a text made from a scan of a copy in the BnF's collection, the source credit printed with it is: Source gallica.bnf.fr / Bibliothèque nationale de France. The statement of rights printed with it reads: The work is in the public domain. The project claims no rights in this text. The digitisation is the BnF's: its published conditions allow non-commercial reuse free of charge with the source credit above; commercial reuse requires a licence from the BnF. For a text made from an Internet Archive item, the credit names the item and its contributor, and the statement of rights gives the public-domain statement that the Archive's record of the scan carries.

The public registers of these pages (works, witnesses, studies, comparisons, open questions and the coverage counts) can be downloaded as CSV and JSON files from the data page.

Lessons recorded in the studies

Summary written by a model from the project's records and checked by a second model; not human-verified

In these records, 'contact' and 'contact zone' are the project's working terms for a textual relation that its method registers between passages. They do not mean that Hume met, read or used the book.

Methods that did not work

Summary written by a model from the project's records and checked by a second model; not human-verified

Studies of search methods, of scoring and prioritisation methods, and of models as readers, whose recorded result is negative or not completed.

  • Bridge test of the register gap between Arnauld and Malebranche result: negativestatus: correctedAcross four doctrine-matched triplets Arnauld was consistently closer to Hume than Malebranche was, and removing 18% apparatus from the Malebranche text made its profile worse, not better (24% to 20% as recorded). The record's conclusion is that the register gap is 'a text-level property, not an embedding model deficiency' and not a contamination problem: some documented relations are not visible at this retrieval layer.
  • Capacity test of the fixed top-fifty list result: negativestatus: correctedA well-known locus (personal identity, THN 1.4.6) was absent from the first fifty pairs despite the highest similarity in the corpus and appeared only when the list was extended to one hundred; the extension contained substantive material, not noise. The record's conclusion is that a list of fixed length is fundamentally mismatched to texts whose points of contact are many and dispersed. A wider-net layer was added in response; it extends the fixed list and does not replace it.
  • Tests of a second embedding model for French retrieval result: negativestatus: correctedAs a replacement the second model 'failed': it recovered 8 of 27 positive anchors, although it preserved both validated clusters and reduced negatives by 61.8%. The comparison was later found circular, because the registry had been shaped by the production model. The model was ratified only as a triggered auxiliary lens for high-risk French settings, always to be compared against the full production stack and 'never evidence by itself'.
  • English and French Huet as a cross-language test line result: not completedstatus: closed without a resultThe mapping attempt was stopped and not integrated: an independent review found that it violated a passage-free rule, and even remediated it would have yielded only about two high-confidence anchors. The principal investigator suspended the line on 20 June 2026 and resumed the discovery and close-reading loop.
  • Search for strong pairs suppressed by the output caps result: negativestatus: currentRecorded as 'negative + cautionary': no scored pair reached the range of the one known suppressed positive, and the magnet controls scored as high as the candidates, so a bare passing score is 'the noise floor' at that depth.
