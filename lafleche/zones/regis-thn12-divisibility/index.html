Where the two texts meet and part

AI-assisted summary (written for display from one model analyst's reading; not reviewed by a second reader; not human-verified)

Régis's chapter on quantity and Hume's two sections stand on opposite sides of one school question, the composition of the continuum, and meet at three stations of the standard debate: the objection that infinitely many parts must make an infinite magnitude, with its answer by proportional parts; the exclusion of extended indivisibles; and the definition of penetration. Régis states the objection and gives the proportional-parts answer; Hume reports that answer and rejects it. Régis defends infinite divisibility from geometry; Hume rejects it from the minimum of the imagination and questions the exactness of geometry, where the Régis stretch has nothing.

Pattern. Clustered in two short places (p832–837 and p919–920), not spread over the stretch read. The two strongest links are one step stated twice.

What this does not show

A narrow zone. The analyst conceded that its two strongest links are one step stated twice, that the threshold is reached only by adding two Cartesian commonplaces, and that the one specific step was common property of the divisibility debate.

The analyst's statement of the relation (Régis p920)

AI-assisted reading (one model analyst, one pass; not human-verified)

Régis p920 affirms that every finite part of matter contains infinitely many possible parts and defends this by the claim that infinitely many aliquant or proportional parts can be conceived in a quantity without the quantity being conceived infinite. Hume's THN 1.2.2 ¶2 denies that a finite extension can contain infinitely many parts and, in the same paragraph, reports and rejects as frivolous exactly a proportional-parts defence. The two also share a premise: an extended part cannot be indivisible by nature (Hume on physical points, THN 1.2.4 ¶3).
